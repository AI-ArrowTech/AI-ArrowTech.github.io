Case 01 · Cloud & data

Modernizing a fragmented analytics estate.

A lineage-led migration turned a hard-to-see legacy environment into a sequenced path toward a governed cloud data platform.

Client contextEnterprise data organization
ChallengeLegacy analytics fragmentation
FocusLineage, migration & governance
Delivery modeStrategy through implementation
The case

Public summary. Client identity, internal systems and commercial details have been withheld.

The situation

Years of organic growth had left analytics workloads distributed across a legacy platform, cloud services and team-owned processes. Dependencies were difficult to see. Migration estimates varied because no one could confidently explain which pipelines, reports and consumers moved together.

The risk was not simply technical debt. A poorly sequenced migration could interrupt decision-making across the business while reproducing the same fragmentation on a new platform.

The transformation needed a dependency map before it needed a migration factory.

What we did

We treated lineage as the planning system. A programmatic inventory brought assets and dependencies into a graph model, creating a common view of upstream sources, transformations and downstream consumers.

Core interventions

  • Built an API-based inventory of legacy analytics assets and their relationships.
  • Modeled technical and business dependencies as a navigable graph.
  • Grouped workloads into migration waves based on risk, coupling and value.
  • Defined cloud landing patterns, ownership and minimum governance controls.
  • Used technical spikes to validate difficult integrations before scaling delivery.

Critical design choices

Sequence by dependencyMigration order followed real coupling rather than organizational preference.
Design for coexistenceThe transition path assumed legacy and cloud environments would operate together.
Make ownership explicitEvery migrated domain needed a named owner and observable data contract.
Prove before scalingHigh-risk patterns were validated with focused spikes before becoming standards.

The impact

The program moved from a list of assets to an evidence-based migration system. Teams could see what depended on what, communicate trade-offs in the same language and plan waves with greater confidence. The target platform was not just newer infrastructure; it was a more governable way of operating data.

What this case demonstrates

Large transformations accelerate when architecture is made legible. The strongest deliverable was not a single diagram or pipeline—it was a decision system that connected strategy, sequencing and implementation.
